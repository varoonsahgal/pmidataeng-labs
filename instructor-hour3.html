Understand it · 1 minute

The hour in one breath

  1. The request: Marketing wants "real-time conversion dashboards in Power BI, across all our markets".
  2. The catch: four ordinary words in that sentence each hide an assumption, and two of them are simply false: the data is weekly, and an under-25 segment is illegal in Italy and Indonesia.
  3. What students do: unpack the four words, let Claude draft clarifying questions and then cut them down to five, prove two facts in SQLTools, and write the requirements Marketing never said. No files, no dbt.
  4. What they leave with: five questions, a four-line feasibility gate (one verdict and one number per requirement), and a short list of requirements including the hidden ones.

The line to repeat all hour: align first, build once. Shift left.

Understand it · 3 minutes

The tables, simply

Students run only two queries this hour. These three tables are all they touch.

rst.sell_through 105 Mondays

What it is: weekly retail sales. One row = one distributor, one product, one week. 2,730 rows from 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-29.

Why it matters: every sale_date is a Monday. The data arrives once a week. There is nothing "real-time" to show.

Like a weekly sales report each shop emails in on Monday morning.

clr.consumers 6,000 people

What it is: one row per consumer: JP 3,000 · IT 2,000 · ID 1,000. It carries age_band; '18-24' is the under-25 band.

Why it matters: the under-25 rule. Marketing to under-25s is forbidden in Italy and Indonesia and allowed in Japan.

Like the customer master file: who each person is.

mpl.consent_records 15,052 slips

What it is: marketing permission slips, about 2.5 per person (one per channel: email, sms, push, postal), each with marketing_allowed true or false.

Why it matters: a person counts as "allowed" if at least one of their slips says yes. Under-25s allowed: IT 0, ID 0, by law and by design.

The same drawer of permission slips as Hour 1.

Two more names students will see in prompts

clr.lifecycle_events (the diary of what each consumer did; "conversion" is one of five stages: registration, trial, conversion, retention, churn) appears in the Claude prompts but is not queried. rfr.filings (112 regulatory filings) appears only in Challenge 3 (the old regulatory variant).

Shift left, in one sentence for the room

Picture a project timeline: Request → Design → Build → Test → Release → Operate. Most teams do their checking on the right. Shift left means doing it on the left, before code exists. What moves left is not the test suite. It is the questions. "Which stage counts as a conversion?" costs five minutes at Request and a rebuild at Operate.

Why AI fits this hour

A vague request and a vague prompt fail the same way: the gap gets filled with the most likely guess, silently. In Hour 1 the guess was "latest consent". Claude is excellent at surfacing forks (questions, stakeholders, hidden requirements). It cannot choose between them, because the right choice depends on what Marketing will decide, which only a conversation reveals. Requirements for humans are context for models.

Teach it · gotchas

What you need to know

  • No files, no dbt, no VM folder this hour. Students use Claude Desktop (fresh chat, Sonnet) and SQLTools only. Nothing lands anywhere; what they keep is what they write down.
  • Nobody opens Power BI. "Power BI" is one of the four words to question, not a tool to use.
  • Use the three verdict phrases exactly: buildable as asked buildable with a change not as asked. Never "no". "Not as asked" always comes with what is buildable.
  • Proof B now counts people, not slips. It uses exists: "does this person have at least one yes slip?" Japan is 319 of 359 (89%). If anyone remembers the older 73%, that counted consent rows (Hour 1's fan-out again); people is the honest unit. What matters for legality is zero vs not zero.
  • Claude will probably leave out the governance stakeholder (Legal / Regulatory / the data owner) in Step 1, and the as-of date in Step 4. Both omissions are the lesson; add them aloud.
  • Claude may jump to design or SQL despite "no design and no SQL yet". Reply in the chat: "No SQL yet. Questions only." That is shift left applied to the assistant.
  • Sonnet all hour. The judgment (curating, choosing an objective, the verdicts) is the human's, so there is no Opus step.
  • Role cards pull people off mute. Three volunteers: Marketing (defends the request, pushes back on verdicts), Legal (asks the under-25 question), Vendor (pitches a streaming platform at the worst moment). Pick them at 2:11.
  • The parking lot pays off here. Students posted one real request at the 1:55 break (board link in chat, or PARKING: messages). You unpack two of them live at 2:53. Pick them during Step 4.

Canned SQL

Paste the whole block into one SQLTools file before class; select one query at a time and press Ctrl+E Ctrl+E. Read-only: nothing here changes the warehouse.

-- Proof A · "real-time"? Sales arrive WEEKLY        expect 2,730 / 105 / 0 / 2024-01-01 / 2025-12-29
select count(*) as rows,
       count(distinct sale_date) as weeks,
       count(*) filter (where to_char(sale_date, 'Dy') <> 'Mon') as not_monday,
       min(sale_date) as first_week,
       max(sale_date) as last_week
from rst.sell_through;

-- Proof B · "all markets"? Under-25 PEOPLE allowed   expect ID 191/0 · IT 249/0 · JP 359/319
select c.market_code,
       count(*) as under25_people,
       count(*) filter (where exists (
           select 1
           from mpl.consent_records m
           where m.consumer_id = c.consumer_id
             and m.marketing_allowed)) as allowed
from clr.consumers c
where c.age_band = '18-24'
group by c.market_code
order by c.market_code;

-- Gate evidence · conversions exist in every market   expect ID 256 · IT 794 · JP 1,348
select market_code, count(*) as conversions
from clr.lifecycle_events
where lifecycle_stage = 'conversion'
group by 1 order by 1;

Challenge solutions

The student page shows each task, a hint and the expected answer. The SQL lives here and in horizon-warehouse/solutions/challenges/hour3/. Every number was run against the warehouse on 2026-10-25.

★ 1 · What is the as-of date?

Expect sales 2025-12-29 conversions 2025-12-31 consent 2025-10-29.

select 'rst.sell_through'     as source, max(sale_date)    as as_of from rst.sell_through
union all
select 'clr.lifecycle_events',           max(event_date)           from clr.lifecycle_events
union all
select 'mpl.consent_records',            max(consent_date)         from mpl.consent_records;
Say

"Three sources, three as-of dates, and consent is two months behind the rest. One 'as of' stamp for the whole dashboard would be wrong for at least one source. That is a requirement nobody would ever have said out loud."

★★ 2 · Prove the weekly view, then spot the hole

The view: 292 rows. A complete chart needs 105 weeks × 3 markets = 315. Weeks with zero conversions: JP 3 · IT 7 · ID 13 (23 in all).

-- (a) the view: one row per week per market                expect 292 rows
select date_trunc('week', event_date)::date as week,
       market_code,
       count(*) as conversions
from clr.lifecycle_events
where lifecycle_stage = 'conversion'
group by 1, 2
order by 1, 2;

-- (b) the hole: weeks with no conversions                   expect ID 13 · IT 7 · JP 3
with weeks as (
  select generate_series('2024-01-01'::date, '2025-12-29'::date, interval '7 days')::date as week),
markets as (select market_code from ref.markets)
select m.market_code,
       count(*) filter (where not exists (
         select 1 from clr.lifecycle_events e
         where e.market_code = m.market_code and e.lifecycle_stage = 'conversion'
           and date_trunc('week', e.event_date)::date = w.week)) as weeks_with_zero
from weeks w cross join markets m
group by 1 order by 1;
Say

"The query is right and the chart is still wrong: a missing week is skipped, not drawn as zero. The fix is a list of every week joined to the counts. And it becomes a requirement: every week appears, zero included. It is also Hour 2's lesson in a new costume: missing rows raise no error."

★★ 3 · The "compliance posture" request

Blended: 50% (56 of 112). Per market: ID 14% (5 of 36, BPOM) IT 65% (24 of 37, AAMS/EU) JP 69% (27 of 39, MHLW).

-- (a) one blended number                                   expect 50
select round(100.0 * count(*) filter (where status = 'accepted') / count(*)) as pct_accepted
from rfr.filings;

-- (b) per market
select market_code,
       string_agg(distinct regulator, ', ') as regulator,
       count(*) filter (where status = 'accepted') as accepted,
       count(*) as filings,
       round(100.0 * count(*) filter (where status = 'accepted') / count(*)) as pct_accepted
from rfr.filings
group by market_code
order by market_code;
Say

"Fifty percent sounds like 'half way there'. Per market it's two markets doing fine and one far behind, with a different regulator each. Verdict on one number: buildable with a change. One number per market, each with its regulator and its as-of date."

★★★ 4 · Unpack your own request, then let Claude grade you

No single answer. What a strong result looks like:

  • Four circled words that are genuinely ambiguous (time words, scope words, "all", "real-time", "simple", "automated", tool names).
  • Five questions that each pass the rule: a different answer changes the build.
  • Claude names at least one hidden requirement (often the as-of date, access, residency or retention) and one forgotten governor (Legal, the data owner, a regulator).
  • A verdict in one of the three phrases, with a number or a fact behind it.
Watch for

Students pasting confidential names or data into the prompt. The request's wording is enough. And if Claude calls every question perfect, the student should ask it to be stricter: "Claude generates, you curate" works both ways.
