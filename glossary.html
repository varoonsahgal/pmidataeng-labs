Before the words

Your kit, in one paragraph

The one rule runs through all four hours. This page explains every other word.

The assistant drafts. The warehouse decides. The engineer signs.

This glossary is for data engineers who may never have used dbt, SQLTools or Claude Desktop before today. Every entry has three parts: what it means, the plain-English version (often an analogy), and where you meet it during the day. Within each group the terms are in alphabetical order.

Numbers in bold are facts from the HORIZON warehouse. Each one has a query you can run yourself in SQLTools; the queries sit in the Prove it section further down this page, grouped as in the glossary, with the expected number as a comment.

Everything you use today is already on your Windows VM:

1 · PMI and Project HORIZON

Accenture LearnVantage
Accenture's learning and training business, the delivery partner for this program. Plain English: The training partner whose name is on the cover slide. Where you meet it: Cover
Adult consumer · adult smoker
PMI's standard phrase: smoke-free products are marketed only to adults who would otherwise continue to smoke. Plain English: The reason every under-25 rule in HORIZON exists. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 3, 4
Age band · under-25 rule
clr.consumers.age_band; '18-24' is the under-25 band. Marketing to under-25s is forbidden in Italy and Indonesia (0% marketing-allowed by design) and allowed in Japan (319 of Japan's 359 under-25 people have at least one "yes" slip, 89%). Plain English: A legal line drawn in the data. In IT and ID the count of under-25 marketing-allowed must always be zero, and a count of zero is a test you can write. Where you meet it: Hour 1 (singular test), Hour 3 (feasibility), Hour 4 (probe and grounding)
analytics (schema)
The schema dbt builds your models into: analytics.italy_conversion_events, analytics.japan_sell_through. Plain English: The output shelf. Raw data lives in clr, mpl, rst, det, rfr; what you build lands in analytics. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
CLR · Consumer Lifecycle Records
clr.consumers (6,000 rows) and clr.lifecycle_events (15,597 rows). Plain English: Who the consumers are, and what happened to them, one event per row. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 3, 4
Consent · marketing permission
mpl.consent_records: marketing_allowed (true or false) plus consent_date. 15,052 rows for 6,000 consumers = 2.51 per consumer, because it is a ledger. Plain English: A consumer's yes or no to marketing, with the date they said it. People change their minds, so each consumer has several rows; only the row in effect on the event's date counts. Where you meet it: Hour 1
Conversion
In PMI usage, an adult smoker switching to a smoke-free product. In HORIZON, the 'conversion' value of lifecycle_stage in clr.lifecycle_events; Italy has 794 conversion events. Plain English: The moment the business cares about most, and the word Marketing never defines in Hour 3. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 3
CoP · DI&A CoP Leads
Community of Practice. The DI&A CoP Leads selected the four skills in this class from a task analysis of the Data Engineer role. Plain English: The people to ask when an internal acronym (DCA, MCTS, REEV) is not spelled out. Where you meet it: Intro
DCA
A PMI-internal system named in your decks ("a DCA classification scope", "a DCA classification job"); ask your CoP lead for the expansion. Plain English: When you see it, read "one of our internal systems". Where you meet it: Icebreakers, Hour 3 Monday artefact
DET · Device Experience Telemetry
det.device_telemetry: 35,113 rows from IQOS and VEEV devices (2 products); 7,084 rows (20%) have a NULL consumer_id on purpose. Plain English: What the devices report back. One row in five is anonymised by design: a privacy measure, not a defect, and a fact any new capability must preserve. Where you meet it: Hour 4
DI&A
Data, Insights & Analytics: PMI's data function and the sponsor of this program. Plain English: The department running this. Where you meet it: Cover, certificate
Distributor · channel type
rst.distributors (12 rows): the trading partner that sells to consumers. channel_type is branded_retail (PMI's own stores), partner_retail (third-party retail) or ecommerce. Plain English: The shop. Both Hour 2 bugs are filters on channel_type that drop one whole distributor. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2, 4
Field force
PMI's sales representatives who visit retail outlets. Plain English: The people on the road; "an MCTS field-force dashboard" is their tool. Where you meet it: Hour 3
Future Skills Initiative · DI&A Enablement Program
PMI's upskilling program: four forward-looking skills per role track, delivered as instructor-led hours, ending in a certificate and badge. Plain English: Why you are here today. Where you meet it: Intro, close
Horizon!Lab2026
The training-only password of the postgres user on your VM. Never a real credential. Plain English: Already saved in the HORIZON connection in SQLTools; you only type it if you re-create the connection. Never paste it into Claude Desktop (see prompt hygiene). Where you meet it: Before you start, every hour
IDP · Individual Development Plan
Where you add the four skills after the program and agree an action plan with your line manager. Plain English: Your four Monday artefacts are its first entries. Where you meet it: Intro, close
IQOS
PMI's heated-tobacco device brand: it heats tobacco sticks (TEREA, HEETS) instead of burning them. A brand name, not an acronym. Plain English: One of the two device products in det.device_telemetry. Where you meet it: Hour 4
Lifecycle stages
registration → trial → conversion → retention → churn: the consumer journey recorded in CLR. Plain English: Five chapters of one consumer's story; each event row is one chapter. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 3
Market · market code · market maturity
A country operating unit and how far its smoke-free transition has gone: JP Japan (mature), IT Italy (mid-adoption), ID Indonesia (early-stage). Plain English: Three countries, three rulebooks. Nothing is "all markets" until you have checked each one. Where you meet it: Everywhere
MCTS
A PMI-internal system named in your decks ("an MCTS store-coverage ask", "an MCTS brief"); ask your CoP lead for the expansion. Plain English: Read "the trade and field-force system" until confirmed. Where you meet it: Icebreakers, Hour 3
MPL · Marketing Permission Ledger
mpl.consent_records, 15,052 rows. Plain English: The consent history book. See ledger in group 3. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 3, 4
PMI
Philip Morris International, the client. Not the Project Management Institute. Plain English: The company whose data this is. Where you meet it: Everywhere
PMI DI&A Future Skills Certificate
The completion certificate for the Data Engineer track, with a shareable digital badge. Plain English: The certificate marks the four skills; the habits are what the next twelve months are for. Where you meet it: Close
Project HORIZON
A fictional, training-only PMI initiative: one Postgres database called horizon with five data assets across three markets. No relation to the UK Post Office's Horizon system, which Hour 2 uses as a cautionary story. Plain English: Today's sandbox. Every number in the class comes from it, and you can check every one. Where you meet it: Everywhere
REEV
A PMI-internal system named in your decks ("a REEV integration", "a REEV mapping"); ask your CoP lead for the expansion. Plain English: Read "one of our internal systems". Where you meet it: Icebreakers, Hour 3
ref.*
Reference tables: ref.markets, ref.products, ref.age_bands, ref.campaigns. Plain English: The lookup lists. Where you meet it: Hour 3 (ref.markets for regions)
Regulatory filing · acceptance
A submission to a regulator about a product, and whether it was accepted. rfr.filings.status is accepted, under_review, submitted or remediation_required. Plain English: Paperwork sent to the regulator, and the regulator's answer. Where you meet it: Hour 3 variant
RFR · Regulatory Filing Repository
rfr.filings, 112 filings, each with a market, a regulator and a status. Plain English: The filing cabinet for regulators. Where you meet it: Hour 3 variant
RST · Retail Sell-Through Feed
rst.sell_through (2,730 weekly rows, 105 distinct Mondays, 0 non-Mondays, 2024-01-01 → 2025-12-29) and rst.distributors (12). Plain English: What the shops sold, week by week. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2, 3, 4

Sell-through: retail sales from the distributor or retailer to the end consumer. Sell-in: PMI's shipments to the distributor. In HORIZON, one row per distributor × product × week; every sale_date is a Monday. Plain English: Sell-in fills the shelf; sell-through empties it. A weekly feed can never be "real-time". Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2, 3
Smoke-free transformation · smoke-free products
PMI's strategic shift away from cigarettes to products without combustion: heated tobacco (IQOS), e-vapor (VEEV) and oral nicotine (ZYN). HORIZON is the fictional data estate behind that shift. Commercial terms: the share of target retail outlets served or visited, and the top-priority tier of those outlets. Plain English: How much of the market the field force reaches. Where you meet it: Hour 3
VEEV
PMI's e-vapor brand. rst.sell_through carries VEEV product codes; the Hour 4 question is "which Japan distributor sells the most VEEV units per week on average?" WA3903 · WA3904 · WA3908 · WA3906
The vendor course codes: Data Engineering with AI Support; Accountability in Automation; Cross-Functional Co-Design & Feasibility Alignment; Adaptive Learning Mindset for Data Engineers. Plain English: Hours 1, 2, 3 and 4, in that order. Where you meet it: Dividers

2 · Markets and regulators

AAMS / EU
Amministrazione Autonoma dei Monopoli di Stato, Italy's former state-monopolies authority for tobacco, since merged into ADM (Agenzia delle Dogane e dei Monopoli). The "/EU" is there because EU-level rules (the Tobacco Products Directive) also apply in Italy. Plain English: Italy's tobacco regulator, plus Brussels. Filing acceptance in HORIZON: 65%. Where you meet it: Hour 3 variant
APAC · EU
Asia-Pacific and European Union: the two regions in ref.markets. Japan and Indonesia are APAC; Italy is EU. Plain English: Two regions, but three regulators; region is not the unit of compliance. Where you meet it: Hour 3 variant
BPOM
Badan Pengawas Obat dan Makanan, Indonesia's National Agency of Drug and Food Control. Plain English: Indonesia's regulator. Filing acceptance in HORIZON: 14%. Where you meet it: Hour 3 variant
Compliance posture
The regulatory team's phrase for "how compliant are we overall". Plain English: HORIZON shows it cannot honestly be one blended number: 14% / 65% / 69% across three regulators. Per market is the only honest design. Where you meet it: Hour 3 variant
Data residency
The requirement that data about a market's consumers stays in an approved jurisdiction. Plain English: Italian consumer data may not be allowed to sit on a server outside the EU. One of the non-functional requirements Marketing never states. Where you meet it: Hour 3 Step 4
ID · Indonesia
Early-stage market, APAC, regulator BPOM. Under-25 marketing forbidden: 0% marketing-allowed. Plain English: The market with the most complex regulation and the lowest filing acceptance. Where you meet it: Hour 1 Challenge 4, Hours 3, 4
IT · Italy
Mid-adoption market, EU, regulator AAMS/EU. Under-25 marketing forbidden: 0%. Plain English: The market in the Hour 1 model and the Hour 2 dashboard complaint. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2, 3, 4
JP · Japan
Mature market, APAC, regulator MHLW. Under-25s allowed: 319 of 359 under-25 people (89%). Plain English: The market nobody noticed was broken in Hour 2, and the one the Hour 4 agent answers about. Where you meet it: Hours 2, 3, 4
MHLW
Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. Plain English: Japan's regulator. Filing acceptance in HORIZON: 69%. Where you meet it: Hour 3 variant
Regulator
The government body that approves products and marketing in a market. Plain English: Three markets, three regulators, three rulebooks. Where you meet it: Hour 3

3 · SQL, dbt and data engineering

Aggregate
A count, sum or average over many rows, returned instead of the rows themselves. Plain English: Ask for the answer, not the rows: one number per distributor is safe to paste into a chat; 35,113 telemetry rows are not. Where you meet it: Hour 4 Step 2
Anomaly threshold
A tolerance band around a series' own average that flags unusual weeks. Japan's real weekly spread is 0.41x to 1.57x of each series' average; on the fixed model a 0.5x/2x rule flags 6 normal weeks; 0.35x/1.75x flags 0. Plain English: Set the alarm just outside what normal looks like, measured against the data, never guessed or pasted from the assistant. Where you meet it: Hour 2 Challenge 3
As-of date
The date the source data was last loaded, printed on every number. Plain English: "Refreshed each morning" describes the report. The as-of date describes the data. Where you meet it: Hour 3
Batch-fed vs real-time
Batch: data arrives in scheduled loads (nightly, weekly). Real-time: each event arrives within seconds through a stream. HORIZON is batch-fed: rst.sell_through has 105 weekly periods. Plain English: A weekly feed cannot power a live dashboard, however often the dashboard refreshes. Where you meet it: Hour 3
Clarifying question
A question whose answer changes what you build. Plain English: If every possible answer leads to the same build, do not ask it. Where you meet it: Hour 3 Step 2
COALESCE
Returns the first non-NULL value in its list: coalesce(marketing_allowed, false). Plain English: "If it is unknown, use this default instead." Always add a comment saying why that default is right. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
Contract
A rule the data must always obey: one row per event, marketing_allowed never NULL. Plain English: A test is a contract written down; dbt checks it on every run. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
CTE · WITH clause
Common Table Expression: a WITH clause that names a sub-query at the top of a statement so the main SELECT below can use it like a table. Plain English: A named scratch table that lives for one query. Where you meet it: Hour 2
dbt (data build tool)
A command-line tool that turns SQL SELECT files into views or tables and tests them. dbt debug checks the connection; dbt run builds; dbt test checks. --select name limits either command to one model. Plain English: A build system for SQL. You write the SELECT; dbt does the CREATE VIEW and runs the checks. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
dbt model
One SQL SELECT saved as a file in models/; dbt run builds it as a view or table. In our labs the first line is {{ config(materialized='view', schema='analytics') }}. Plain English: One file = one view. Hour 1 creates models/analytics/italy_conversion_events.sql; Hour 2 edits models/japan_sell_through.sql. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
dbt source
A declared raw table, written {{ source('schema','table') }}, for example {{ source('mpl','consent_records') }}. Plain English: How a model points at a raw table without hard-coding its name. The project file models/_horizon_sources.yml already declares every HORIZON schema (rst, clr, mpl, det, rfr, ref), so any {{ source('schema','table') }} compiles with no setup. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
dbt ref()
{{ ref('japan_sell_through') }} points at another dbt model you built. Plain English: source() for raw tables, ref() for your own models. Where you meet it: Hour 2 tests
DISTINCT ON (Postgres)
select distinct on (key) ... order by key, consent_date desc keeps exactly one row per key: the first by the ORDER BY. Plain English: "For each event, keep only the newest consent dated on or before it." The one-line fix for a fan-out. Where you meet it: Hour 1 corrected model
DSN · connection string
postgres://user:password@host:5432/db: the database address and keys in one line. Plain English: Your SQLTools connection holds one. Never paste it into Claude Desktop. Where you meet it: Hour 4 (instructor's MCP demo)
Fan-out
A join that multiplies rows because the right side has several matches per key. 794 Italy conversion events joined to the consent ledger became 1,988 rows. Plain English: One event, 2.5 consent rows, 2.5 copies of the event. The row count is how you catch it. Where you meet it: Hour 1
Feasibility verdict
The three allowed answers to a request: buildable as asked · buildable with a change · not as asked. Plain English: Never a plain yes or no. Always one of three, with a warehouse number as evidence. Where you meet it: Hour 3
Generic tests · schema.yml
Tests declared in YAML against a model's columns: not_null, unique, relationships (every value exists in another table), accepted_values (only these values). Plain English: Four reusable checks you switch on by naming the column. Hour 1 ends with 6 generic tests + 1 singular test = 7 passes. Where you meet it: Hour 1 Step 4
Grain
What one row represents: one row per conversion event; one row per distributor, product and week. Plain English: Before you join anything, finish the sentence "one row is one ___". If a join changes the answer, it changed the grain. Where you meet it: Everywhere
INNER vs LEFT (vs FULL) join
INNER keeps only rows that match on both sides. LEFT keeps every left-side row and fills the right side with NULL when nothing matches. FULL keeps both sides. Plain English: INNER hides the rows you are hunting; a reconciliation must be LEFT or FULL. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
IS DISTINCT FROM
A comparison that treats NULL as a value: campaign_id is distinct from 1 returns 15,523 rows where campaign_id <> 1 returns 0. Plain English: "Different from 1, and yes, unknown counts as different." Where you meet it: Hour 2 opener
**Jinja · {{ }}**
dbt's templating. Anything in double curly braces (config, source, ref) is replaced before the SQL runs. Plain English: The curly braces are instructions to dbt, not to Postgres. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
Join condition
The ON clause of a join. A filter hidden inside it (and d.channel_type <> 'ecommerce') silently drops rows. Plain English: Read the whole join, not just the key. Where you meet it: Hour 2 Diagnose
KPI · KPI-level check
Key Performance Indicator; a check on a business number ("under-25 marketing in Italy is always zero") rather than on a job status. Plain English: A green job status says "it ran". A KPI check says "the number is right". Where you meet it: Hour 2 takeaway
Ledger
A table that keeps history: many rows per key over time, rather than one current row per key. mpl.consent_records has 2.51 rows per consumer. Plain English: A bank statement, not a balance. Joining a ledger directly fans out; joining it point-in-time does not. Where you meet it: Hour 1
NFR · non-functional requirement
Refresh cadence, cost, reliability, access control, data residency, regulatory constraint, as-of date. Plain English: The requirements nobody says out loud and everybody assumes. Where you meet it: Hour 3 Step 4
NULL · three-valued logic
SQL comparisons return true, false or unknown; WHERE keeps only true. campaign_id <> 1 returns 0 rows, not 15,523, because NULL <> 1 is unknown. Plain English: NULL is "I don't know". "I don't know ≠ 1" is still "I don't know", and the filter throws it away without a word. Where you meet it: Hour 2 opener, Hour 1
Orphan row
A fact row whose key has no match in the dimension (a sell-through row for a distributor missing from rst.distributors). An INNER join drops it silently; a LEFT join keeps it with NULLs; a relationships test catches it. Indonesia today: 0 orphans, which is exactly why the INNER-join bug ships. Plain English: A delivery note for a shop nobody has on file. Where you meet it: Hour 1 Challenge 4 (Indonesia)
Point-in-time
The record in effect on the event's date, not the latest one. "Latest consent per consumer" stamps 31 of 794 Italy events with a consent dated after the event. Plain English: What did the consumer say at the time, not what they say now. Where you meet it: Hour 1
Postgres · PostgreSQL · schema
The database engine behind horizon. A schema is a named folder of tables: clr, mpl, rst, det, rfr, ref, analytics. Plain English: clr.consumers reads "the consumers table in the clr folder". Where you meet it: Everywhere
Power BI Desktop vs Service
Desktop is the local report-authoring app a single analyst installs; Service is the cloud workspace with scheduled refresh and sharing. In the HORIZON scenario Marketing has Desktop only, so a self-refreshing dashboard is not available as asked. Plain English: A dashboard that refreshes itself needs Service. With Desktop only, you share a file. Where you meet it: Hour 3
Reconciliation
Comparing the pipeline's output to its source per key (here per distributor) to find rows that vanished. IT Partner Distributor 2: 210 in / 0 out. JP Ecommerce Distributor 3: 105 / 0. Start from the list of what should exist (here rst.distributors), so a missing key shows up as a 0. Plain English: Count on both sides of the pipe, per distributor, and subtract. Where you meet it: Hour 2 Detect
Regression
An old bug coming back after a later change. Plain English: The test you write today is what fails the build when someone brings the bug back next month. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
Request vs specification
A request says what someone wants, in their words ("real-time"). A specification says exactly what will be built, in terms you can test ("refreshed by 07:00 each morning, as-of date shown on every number"). Plain English: A request is intent, not a specification. Where you meet it: Hour 3
Shift left
Do the checking at Request and Design, before any code exists, instead of at Test and Operate. Plain English: The thing that moves left is the questions. Where you meet it: Hour 3
Silent suppression
Rows removed by a rule with no error and no alert. Plain English: The pipeline is green; the distributor is gone. Where you meet it: Hour 2
Singular test
A SQL file in tests/ that returns rows only on failure; dbt test fails if any row comes back. Examples: tests/no_underage_marketing_italy.sql, tests/assert_japan_no_dropped_distributors.sql. Plain English: A smoke alarm. Silence means fine; one row means the build goes red. Hour 2 makes you trip it on purpose so you know it works. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
SQLTools
The VS Code extension used to run SQL against HORIZON. Saved connection HORIZON: localhost / 5432 / horizon / postgres / Horizon!Lab2026. Plain English: Your window into the warehouse. Paste a query into a SQLTools query tab, select it and press Ctrl+E Ctrl+E (or click the Run on active connection link that appears above the query). Where you meet it: Every hour
Staged · staging model
The first dbt layer built over a source: analytics.japan_sell_through is the staged version of rst.sell_through for Japan. "Source vs staged" means raw vs built. Japan: 945 rows planted, 1,050 fixed. Plain English: The pipe's output, as opposed to its input. Where you meet it: Hour 2
Stakeholder
Anyone who asks for, uses, pays for or governs the thing you build. Plain English: Not only whoever sent the request: Legal and the regulatory owner count too. Where you meet it: Hour 3 Step 1
Superuser
A database login that can read and change everything. postgres, your SQLTools login, is one. Plain English: Fine for a training sandbox; never the login an agent should connect with. Where you meet it: Hour 4 Step 4
Threshold drift
An alert threshold that no longer matches the data and so never fires (or fires constantly). Plain English: A smoke alarm with a dead battery. Where you meet it: Hour 2
View
A saved SELECT that behaves like a table but stores nothing; our dbt models are views in analytics. Plain English: A window onto the data, re-run every time you query it. Where you meet it: Hours 1, 2
Window function
row_number() over (partition by event_id order by consent_date desc) numbers rows within a group; an alternative to DISTINCT ON for "one row per key". Plain English: Rank inside each group, keep rank 1. Where you meet it: Hour 1 Step 3 (the reference model)
YAML
The indented text format of schema.yml. Plain English: Indentation is meaning: two spaces, never tabs. Where you meet it: Hour 1 Step 4

4 · AI terms: Claude Desktop, models and agents

Agent
A model, plus a set of tools, plus a loop (think, call a tool, read the result, repeat), plus whatever permissions it was given. Plain English: In Hour 4 the "tool" is you: Claude writes the SQL, you run it in SQLTools and decide what to paste back. Every tool call is a human running a query. Where you meet it: Hour 4
Allow once · Allow always
The two choices on the permission prompt in the instructor's MCP demo (labels vary by version). Plain English: "Allow always" removes the gate for good. Choose it and the agent never asks again. Where you meet it: Hour 4 Step 3 (instructor-led)
Automation bias
Accepting a confident, specific suggestion from a tool more readily than from a colleague, and checking it less. Plain English: A threshold you would never accept from a junior engineer, pasted from Claude without a check. Where you meet it: Hour 2 Challenge 3
Blast radius
How much damage a tool or job could do if it is wrong: zero for a read-only door, large for an unattended nightly delete. Plain English: Ask "what is the worst this can do?" before "how good is the demo?". Where you meet it: Hours 2, 4
Claude Desktop
The Claude chat app on your VM. It answers in a chat window; it cannot create or edit files on the VM. Start every lab with New chat. Plain English: Claude types the draft; you carry it into the repo. The paste is you, signing. Where you meet it: Every hour
Connectors · MCP server
In Claude Desktop, the + button at the bottom-left of the chat box → Connectors lists the tools Claude may call. In the instructor's Hour 4 demo a read-only database door named horizon is connected. Your VM has no connectors, by design. Plain English: A tool plugged into the chat. Watch the demo: the permission prompt, the tool call, the SQL, and the DELETE the tool refuses. Where you meet it: Hour 4 Step 3 (instructor-led)
Context window
