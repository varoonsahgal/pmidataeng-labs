The big picture · read this first

The mission in 60 seconds

One sentence from Marketing, four hidden assumptions, and the two numbers that decide what can be built.

The situation

Marketing sends you this request:

"Can you build us real-time conversion dashboards in Power BI, across all our markets, so we can watch the smoke-free transition as it happens?"

It sounds clear. It is not. Each of the four words in bold hides a guess about how things work, and two of those guesses are simply wrong. Build it exactly as asked and you get a project that cannot work, or one that breaks the law.

Your mission

Before anyone writes a line of code, turn the request into a plan that Marketing and engineering both agree on.

You will not build anything this hour. You will find out what should be built.

The two numbers that decide this hour

"Real-time"?105

Sales data arrives on 105 Mondays, once a week, never in between. A dashboard cannot be fresher than the data feeding it.

"All markets"?0

Under-25s in Italy and Indonesia who may be marketed to: zero. The law forbids it. An under-25 view across all markets would be illegal in two of three.

You will prove both numbers yourself in Step 3, with two short queries.

The theme

What "shift left" means

Catch the mistake before the code exists, because that is when it is cheapest.

Every project moves from left to right along a timeline: Request → Design → Build → Test → Operate. The further right a mistake is found, the more it costs to fix, because more has been built on top of it. "Shift left" means moving the checking towards the start: asking the questions before anyone builds.

You have already seen this today. All three hours had the same kind of mistake: a guess nobody checked.

HourThe unchecked guessWhere it was caughtCost
Hour 1"attach the consent record" (there were up to four)in review, 25 minutes after the drafta rewrite of one model
Hour 2"ecommerce is not a store, so drop it"in production, months later105 missing rows on every dashboard
Hour 3"real-time", "all markets"…now, before any codefive questions

What moves left is not the testing. It is the questions.

Six words for this hour

Everything else is in the glossary.

Six words Full glossary →
WordPlain English
request vs specificationA request says what someone wants, in their words ("real-time"). A specification says exactly what will be built, so you can test it ("refreshed by 07:00 every morning").
stakeholderAnyone who asks for, uses, pays for or governs the thing. The one everybody forgets is the one who governs it (Legal, the data owner).
feasibility verdictOur answer to one requirement. Only three are allowed: buildable as asked buildable with a change not as asked, each with a number from the warehouse.
functional vs non-functionalFunctional: what it shows ("weekly conversions per market"). Non-functional: how it must behave (how fresh, who may see it, which law applies). Nobody says the second kind. Everybody expects it.
batch vs real-timeBatch: data arrives in scheduled loads (nightly, weekly). Real-time: each event arrives within seconds. HORIZON is batch.
as-of dateThe date the data was last loaded, printed next to every number, so nobody mistakes last Monday's sales for today's.

AProof A · the word "real-time"

"How often does sales data actually arrive?"

Why we run it

A dashboard can only be as fresh as its data. If sales arrived every few seconds, "real-time" would be possible. If they arrive once a week, it is not.

Run this 
select count(*) as rows,
       count(distinct sale_date) as weeks,
       count(*) filter (where to_char(sale_date, 'Dy') <> 'Mon') as not_monday,
       min(sale_date) as first_week,
       max(sale_date) as last_week
from rst.sell_through;
Line by line
count(*) as rowsHow many rows of sales are there in total?
count(distinct sale_date) as weeksHow many different dates? Each date is one delivery of data.
count(*) filter (where to_char(sale_date, 'Dy') <> 'Mon')Count only the rows whose date is not a Monday. (to_char(…, 'Dy') turns a date into its day name, like Mon; <> means "is not".)
min(sale_date) / max(sale_date)The first and the last delivery.
from rst.sell_through…in the weekly sales table.

The three verdicts, and why there is no "no"

buildable as asked we build what they said. buildable with a change we build something close, and say exactly what changes. not as asked we cannot build it that way, and here is what we can build instead. A bare "no" ends the conversation; these three keep it going.

★☆☆

1 · What is the as-of date?

5 min

The question: the dashboard will read from three tables. What date would you print next to each one's numbers? Find the newest date in rst.sell_through (sale_date), clr.lifecycle_events (event_date) and mpl.consent_records (consent_date).

Hint

select max(sale_date) from rst.sell_through; gives the newest date in one table. Do the same for the other two, or glue the three answers into one result with union all.

Check your answer

Sales 2025-12-29 · conversions 2025-12-31 · consent 2025-10-29. Three tables, three different as-of dates. The consent data is two months older than the rest, so a single "as of" stamp on the whole dashboard would mislead. That is a hidden requirement nobody said: one as-of date per source.

★★☆

2 · Prove the weekly view, then spot the hole

10 min

The question: the gate says "weekly conversions per market" is buildable as asked. Prove it with one query: one row per week, per market, with the number of conversions. Then look closely. Is anything missing?

Hint

date_trunc('week', event_date) turns any date into the Monday of its week. Filter to lifecycle_stage = 'conversion', then group by the week and market_code. Count your result rows. The data covers 105 weeks and 3 markets: how many rows would a complete chart need?

Check your answer

The query returns 292 rows, but a complete chart needs 105 × 3 = 315. Weeks with zero conversions simply vanish: JP 3, IT 7, ID 13. On a line chart, a missing week is silently skipped, not shown as zero. Another hidden requirement: every week appears, zero included.
